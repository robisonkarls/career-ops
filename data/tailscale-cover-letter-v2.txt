Dear Tailscale Team,

I'm writing to express my strong interest in the Software Engineer, Strategic Projects 
role at Tailscale. Your team's explicit embrace of coding agents as a force multiplier 
represents exactly where I believe the next generation of platform engineering is headed. 
I don't just use agents—I've architected agent-native systems at production scale.

At Dayforce, I designed and deployed an SDD (Spec-Driven Development) framework that 
integrated AI skills directly into our core engineering workflow. Here's what this means in 
practice:

**Specification → Planning → Development → Business-Aware Review**

1. **Specification Layer**: Jira user stories as AI-consumable specs, not just documentation
2. **Planning Layer**: AI skills that generate implementation tasks and architectural plans 
   from specs, automatically tailored to our tech stack
3. **Development Layer**: GitHub SpecKit foundation, customized for Dayforce's tooling 
   ecosystem (Jira, GitHub, internal systems)
4. **Review Layer**: AI-powered PR review that understands *business context*. The agent 
   connects code changes back to user story intent, validates business value added (not 
   just syntax correctness), and surfaces insights humans would miss.

**The result:** measurable velocity increase with zero quality loss.

This taught me what most engineers are still learning: building agent-native systems isn't 
about bolting LLMs on top of existing workflows. It's about redesigning the workflow around 
what agents do best—rapid iteration, context synthesis, multi-tool orchestration—while 
maintaining architectural rigor and business alignment.

What excites me about Tailscale's Strategic Projects team is the challenge of scaling this 
thinking to infrastructure. How do agents interact with connectivity? How should MCP gateways 
be designed so agents can safely provision, configure, or audit Tailscale's platform? How 
does your product need to evolve to be "agent-native"? These are the exact problems that 
drew me to systems engineering in the first place.

Throughout my 13+ years in backend systems, I've learned that platform thinking and business 
impact go hand in hand. I've designed identity infrastructure (OAuth, SAML, OIDC) serving 
millions of users. I've shipped distributed systems (Kubernetes, Kafka, microservices) from 
POC to production. I've led teams that moved at startup velocity without sacrificing 
production standards. The Dayforce SDD framework proves I can do all three simultaneously: 
platform architecture + agent integration + measurable business results.

I've also embedded this thinking into career-ops, an open-source job search platform where 
I led the GitHub Copilot integration. It's validation of the SDD framework on a smaller 
scale—proof that the approach transfers across domains.

Go is new territory for me, but systems thinking and platform architecture transfer. I've 
shipped in Java, Python, Rust, and TypeScript. Go's philosophy—simple, pragmatic, production-focused—
resonates deeply with how I think about systems.

I'm excited about the possibility of helping Tailscale define what agent-native, business-aware 
connectivity infrastructure looks like at global scale.

Best regards,  
Robison Karls
